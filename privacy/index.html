Polycule

Privacy policy

Last updated October 28, 2026. Polycule holds some of the most personal information there is: who you love and how. This policy explains what we collect, why, who can see it, where it’s kept, and what you can do about it.

The short version

  • We collect what Polycule needs to work, and nothing for advertising. There are no ads, no analytics or tracking tools, and no location.
  • What you share in a polycule is seen by the people in it, and not by anyone else.
  • We never sell your information or use it to profile you.
  • Your data is stored in the United States, by the service providers listed in section 5.
  • You can delete your account, and its data, at any time in the app.

1. Who we are

Polycule is made and run by Reid Townson, an individual developer in Ontario, Canada. In this policy, “we”, “us” and “our” mean Reid Townson.

We’re responsible for your personal information in Polycule. Reid Townson is also the person in charge of protecting it, and can be reached at support@wobblynoodles.com.

This policy works together with our terms of use.

2. What we collect

What you give us

  • Your account: your email address, your password (stored only in scrambled, “hashed” form, which can’t be turned back into the password), your name and your avatar.
  • Your polycules: which polycules you’re in, the name you go by in each, your connections to other people and the labels on them, people and pets you add, the layout of your polycule’s graph, and invitations you create.
  • What you write and play: answers to questions, game words, drawings and guesses, and your activity in each polycule.
  • Private settings: nicknames you give people, who you’ve muted or blocked, and your notification preferences.
  • Hearts, blossoms and store items: your balances, what you’ve earned and spent, and what you own.
  • Reports: if you report someone, what you tell us.
  • Messages to us: anything you send to our support address.

Sensitive information

Your relationships, and what you write about them, can reveal your sex life and your sexual orientation. Privacy law treats that as sensitive, and so do we. We collect it only because it’s what Polycule is for, we handle it under your express consent, given when you agree to this policy as you create your account, and we never use it for anything else.

What we collect automatically

  • A notification token for each device you sign in on, from Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging, so we can send you notifications.
  • Technical logs. Like any internet service, our servers record requests, including the IP address they came from and when they were made. These are used only for security and to fix problems.
  • Timestamps, such as when you last opened the activity feed or earned your daily heart, so the app can work out what’s new.

From Google Play

When you buy Premium or blossoms, Google takes the payment. We never see your card or bank details. Google tells us what you bought, when, its purchase token, and whether a subscription is active, renewing or cancelled.

What we don’t collect

We don’t collect your location, contacts, photos, microphone, phone number or advertising ID, and the app contains no advertising or analytics tools.

3. How we use it

Only to:

  • run Polycule: your account, your polycules and everything in them;
  • send notifications you’ve allowed, and emails you need, such as a password reset;
  • provide Premium and blossom purchases, and confirm them with Google Play;
  • keep people safe: act on reports, enforce the community guidelines, and prevent abuse and fraud;
  • answer your messages, and fix problems;
  • meet our legal obligations.

We don’t sell your information, rent it, share it for advertising, or use it to build profiles of you. No decisions about you are made by automated means alone. We don’t send marketing emails.

4. Who can see what

  • The people in your polycule see what you share there: your name and avatar, your connections, your answers, game moves and activity. Some question answers stay hidden until people have answered themselves.
  • Everyone in every polycule you’re in sees your account name and avatar, and notices when you change your name.
  • Only you see your private nicknames for people, who you’ve muted or blocked, and your hearts, blossoms and purchases.
  • Only we see reports. The person reported is never told who reported them.
  • Notifications can show names and what happened on your lock screen. You can switch to discreet notifications in Settings, which say only that something is waiting.

We can see the data in our database when we need to, for example to look into a report or a problem you’ve asked us about. We don’t browse it otherwise.

5. Service providers

We use these companies to run Polycule. Supabase, Resend and Firebase handle your information on our behalf, under agreements that require them to protect it and not use it for their own purposes. Google Play and GitHub also handle some information under their own privacy policies, as noted.

  • Supabase hosts our database, sign-in and server functions. Almost everything in section 2 is stored with Supabase.
  • Google delivers notifications through Firebase Cloud Messaging. Payments through Google Play are handled under Google’s own privacy policy.
  • Resend sends our emails, such as password resets. It handles your email address and the content of those emails.
  • Cloudflare forwards email sent to our support address.
  • GitHub hosts this website, and records visitors’ IP addresses for security under its own privacy statement. The site uses no cookies and no analytics.

6. Where your data is kept

Polycule’s database is hosted by Supabase in the United States. Google, Resend, Cloudflare and GitHub may also process data in the United States and other countries.

While your information is in another country, it’s protected by our agreements with these providers, but it’s also subject to that country’s laws. Courts, law enforcement and national security authorities there may be able to access it under those laws.

7. Sharing for legal and safety reasons

Apart from the service providers above, we share personal information only:

  • when the law requires it, such as a valid court order or warrant, and then only what’s required;
  • when we believe someone’s life, health or safety is in danger;
  • to report suspected child sexual abuse or exploitation to Cybertip.ca or the police;
  • to protect our rights in a legal dispute;
  • if Polycule is ever transferred to someone else, such as a company we form. They would be bound by this policy, and we’d tell you first.

8. How long we keep it

  • Your account and content are kept until you delete them or your account. When you delete your account, they’re deleted straight away. This page lists exactly what goes and what stays.
  • Shared things you added, such as people without an account, pets and finished games, stay for the other members of the polycule. They no longer show that you added them.
  • Activity feed lines are kept only until newer ones replace them. Each feed keeps its most recent 20.
  • Backups of the database are overwritten within 30 days.
  • Technical logs are kept by our providers for a short time, usually days, then deleted.
  • Reports are kept after the accounts in them are deleted, including both accounts’ ids, names and email addresses at the time of the report. That’s so a banned person can be recognised if they come back. Reports that led to no action are deleted after two years. Reports that led to a ban are kept while the ban lasts.
  • Messages to support are kept for as long as we need them to help you, and no more than two years.
  • Payment records are kept by Google, under Google’s privacy policy.

9. Keeping it safe

Everything sent between the app and our servers is encrypted, and our database is encrypted where it’s stored. Access rules in the database decide who can read each piece of data, so the app can only fetch what you’re allowed to see. Only we can access the database directly, and our accounts with our providers are protected by strong passwords and two-step sign-in.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach of security puts you at real risk of significant harm, we’ll tell you, and report it to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, as the law requires.

10. Your choices and rights

  • See and correct your information. Most of it you can see and change in the app. For a copy of everything we hold about you, email us from your sign-in address.
  • Delete it. Delete your whole account in the app, or without the app by following these steps. Some things, like question answers, can’t be changed once given. To have one removed without deleting your account, email us.
  • Withdraw your consent. You can do this at any time by deleting your account. We can’t run Polycule for you without the information it needs.
  • Notifications. Turn them off, or make them discreet, in Settings or in your phone’s settings.

We’ll answer requests within 30 days, and free of charge. We may need to confirm the request is really from you, which is why it should come from your sign-in address. If we can’t do what you ask, for example because the law requires us to keep something, we’ll explain why.

Depending on where you live, you may have more rights under your local laws. Email us and we’ll respect them.

11. People who aren’t in the app

Members can add people to a polycule who don’t have an account, with a name, an avatar and their connections to others. If you’re one of those people and want information about you removed, email us and tell us which polycule it’s in. We’ll remove it.

12. Age

Polycule is only for adults: 18, or the age of majority where you live if that’s older. We don’t knowingly collect information from anyone younger. If we learn an account belongs to a minor, we close it and delete its data. If you believe a minor is using Polycule, please report them in the app or email us.

13. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy significantly, especially how we use or share your information, we’ll tell you in the app and ask you to agree again before the change applies to you. Smaller fixes, such as clearer wording, just change the date at the top.

14. Contact and complaints

For any question or request about your privacy, or a complaint, email support@wobblynoodles.com. We’ll look into every complaint and tell you what we find.

If you aren’t satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. In Quebec, you can also contact the Commission d’accès à l’information. If you live elsewhere, you can contact the privacy authority where you live.
